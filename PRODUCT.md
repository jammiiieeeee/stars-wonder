# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Plain static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript served by a small Node.js HTTP server. No framework and no build step. The page must run from `npm start` on the local machine.

## Users

People who open a link to look at something beautiful. They arrive curious, stay for one to three minutes, and leave without a task to complete. There is no account, no data, and no return visit to support.

## Product Purpose

One page that shows a celestial body assembled from glowing particles floating in space. The visitor orbits it, approaches it, and reshapes it. Success means the page feels like a finished piece rather than a technology demo: it holds up at first glance, survives interaction, and leaves a clear memory of the object.

## Positioning

The body is a real-time particle system the visitor physically moves around and reforms, not a video or a static hero image. The interaction is the product.

## Operating Context

Dark room, desktop or phone, full-screen browser. The page is looked at rather than operated, so the object leads and the interface recedes. The visitor may be on a trackpad, a mouse, or a touch screen.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- A single page served by Node.js from a local install.
- Real-time 3D rendering in WebGL through Three.js.
- Orbit and zoom, plus controls for reform, spin speed, and glow.
- Must keep a smooth frame rate on an integrated GPU and a recent phone.
- No backend, no persistence, no external API at runtime.

## Brand Commitments

The name "Stars Wonder" is the working title of the piece. The visitor's chosen subject is a living star: warm gold and champagne particles, a turbulent corona, and a slow pulse.

## Evidence on Hand

None. No photography, no data, no customer claims. Everything rendered is generated by the page itself. Nothing on the page may claim real astronomical data or observation.

## Product Principles

1. The object is the page. Interface chrome exists only where it changes the object.
2. Interaction is authored, not bolted on. Every control has a visible, reversible effect.
3. Finish is measured at the render, on a phone and on a desktop, not in the code.
4. Performance is part of the look. A stutter breaks the illusion faster than a missing detail.
