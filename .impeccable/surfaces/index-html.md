# Stars Wonder — the field

**Scope:** The single page at `/`. **Visitor mode:** Experience. The work leads from the first viewport; the interface is the instrument's collar.

**Audience and job:** Anyone who opens a link to look at something beautiful, in a dark room for one to three minutes. They are not completing a task. They orbit, approach, and reshape by curiosity.

**Action:** Drag or use the arrow keys to orbit. Scroll or use plus/minus to approach. Focus and Tracking change the light and the drift. Release lets the body fall apart; Recollect calls it back. Space does the same from the keyboard.

**Proof and content:** The body itself, generated live. Readouts are real state (particle count, approach, drift). Nothing claims an astronomical observation; the note says so.

**Constraints:** One page, no backend, no runtime API, a local Node static server, WebGL, 48,000 particles on desktop and 22,000 on coarse pointers, one authored reform moment, reduced-motion respected.

## Direction contract

THESIS: The page is one turn at a public observatory: a living star seen through an eyepiece, with the instrument's controls as the only chrome. It refuses the full-bleed space hero and the floating glass control card.

OWN-WORLD: Near-black ink ground and one circular field with a knurled brass bezel. Champagne and gold particle light, a cream core, an ember edge. Bodoni Moda for the piece's name; Archivo for instrument labels, tabular readouts, and verbs. Square corners, hairline rules, no cards, no glass. Amber marks focus; ember marks the released state.

STORY: The visitor sees a body of light, moves around it, brings it closer, and can let it fall apart into a field of stars and call it back. They understand that it is composed on the screen, not photographed.
